Using Seva while you wait

Getting started

1
Pick where you are Hospital, train or bus. This decides what Seva listens for.
2
Type the one thing you are waiting for Your token like A-47, a train number like 12739, or a bus route. Just one. Seva watches that and ignores everything else.
3
Press Start listening and put the phone down Screen up, facing the hall, not buried in a bag. You can lock the screen.
4
Wait for the buzz Three short buzzes mean your number was called. The screen shows it big and reads it aloud.

Keep the phone out of your pocket. Cloth over the microphone is the single most common reason Seva hears nothing.

If something is wrong

Your phone's browser is holding the permission, and no app can change that from inside a page. You have to clear it yourself:

  1. Tap the padlock or sliders icon in the browser address bar.
  2. Open Permissions and set Microphone to Allow.
  3. Come back and press Start listening again.

If there is no padlock, close the tab completely and open Seva again — the browser will ask you fresh.

Check three things, in this order:

  1. The phone's silent switch or Do Not Disturb — it mutes the browser too.
  2. The volume buttons while Seva is open. Phones keep a separate media volume.
  3. Settings → Auto play audio is on.

The number stays on screen either way, and Replay will say it again.

It happens, and Seva is built to fail this way rather than guess. Usually one of:

  1. The phone was face down or in a bag.
  2. The announcement was too far from the speaker to come through cleanly.
  3. The number was said in a form the rules do not accept yet — "token a forty seven" spelled out in words is a known gap.

Seva is a second pair of ears, not a replacement for the board. For anything that matters, still check the display.

What Seva will never do

Show you a number the announcement did not contain.
Invent a doctor's name, a timing, a room or a phone number.
Ask for your name, your phone number or any document.
Keep a recording of the hall after it has been read.
Give medical advice. It repeats the hospital's own written information.
