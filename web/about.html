About Seva

Why it exists and how it works

Seva

An ear that waits for your number, so you do not have to strain to hear it.

The problem

She waits for exactly one piece of information. It is announced once or twice over a bad speaker in a loud hall, mixed with dozens of announcements for other people, possibly in a language she does not follow. If she misses it she loses a day's wages, a seat, or the journey.

Indian Railways' own handbook requires announcements to run 15 to 25 dB above background noise. In a crowded hall they often do not. Seva listens when a person cannot.

How it works

1
You tell it what you are waiting for A token like A-47, a train number, or a bus route. One thing.
2
It listens, and ignores the quiet A loudness gate on the phone only sends audio when someone is actually announcing. An hour of waiting costs about 7.5 minutes of speech recognition.
3
It reads the number out of the sentence Fixed rules, not guesswork. If two train numbers appear in one announcement, it refuses rather than pick one.
4
It buzzes and speaks, in your language Your phone vibrates and says the number aloud, slowly.

The one promise

A missed number is survivable. An invented one is not.

If Seva did not clearly hear the platform, it says so and tells you to check the board. It will never print a number the announcement did not contain.

What is inside

The earGnani Prisma speech-to-text, tuned for noisy halls and biased toward local place names like Tenali and Vijayawada.
The wordsA language model rephrases the hospital's own written facts into a plain answer. It is a rewriter, never a source of facts.
The voiceGnani Timbre v3.0, voice Chetana — chosen because it is the gentle, unhurried one.

Languages

Spoken alerts are checked word for word in three languages today. The rest are in the picker and will follow.
